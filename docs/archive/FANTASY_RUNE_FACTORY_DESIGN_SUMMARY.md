# Fantasy Rune Factory Design Summary

## Product promise

Build readable magical factories that turn limited-throughput resources into rune families, machine upgrades, and advanced magic. New discoveries should create new production relationships rather than merely add more items.

## Player experience

- Shapez-style omniscient camera with no avatar or combat.
- Light players can understand early production through silhouettes, color, and immediate feedback.
- Advanced players optimize ratios, shared inputs, delivery windows, and upgrade allocation using the same deterministic rules.

## Rune language

- Central sigil: magical family or function.
- Element: fire, water, earth, or wind.
- Progression state: base, synthesized, ascended, and possibly layered later.
- Materials and special cores remain candidates for advanced recipes.
- Rotation, Attack/Split glyphs, and left/right elemental zones are not part of the new required production language.

## Progression

- Early: basic spirit and functional runes; engraving, infusion, delivery, and the first socket upgrade.
- Early-mid: more families and simultaneous deliveries sharing resources.
- Mid: celestial or advanced spirit families and synthesis.
- Mid-late: dragons, greater spirits, and ascension with explicit catalysts.
- Late: spirit kings, dragon lords, advanced constellations, and linked supply networks.
- Endgame: stable large-scale production and compound contracts. A universal factory is a player choice, not a mandated feature.

Names, recipe counts, exact tiers, processing times, prices, and unlock order remain provisional.

## Production depth

Depth comes from recipe inputs, meaningful process order, production ratios, and throughput allocation.

Synthesis combines completed runes into a new identity. Ascension evolves a rune using explicit sacrifices or catalysts. Layering preserves multiple identities and is a later candidate.

One machine socket accepts a manufactured rune. Socket effects should visibly alter behavior. Upgrade runes also compete with delivery and advanced crafting demand.

## Resources and contracts

Scarcity should primarily come from extraction points and items per second rather than depletion. Shared resources create allocation problems across rune families.

Campaign introduces one production concept at a time.

Freeplay selects unlocked and manufacturable outputs, then varies:

- cumulative quantity,
- sustained delivery rate,
- simultaneous targets,
- mixed cumulative and sustained conditions.

Sustained-rate goals use actual Hub deliveries over a fair measurement window.

## Implementation sequence

1. Stabilize the existing extraction-to-Hub loop.
2. Assess rotation dependencies and remove mandatory rotation from objectives safely.
3. Prove one rune delivery and one machine socket upgrade end to end.
4. Prove simultaneous deliveries and shared-resource pressure.
5. Add a second family using common processing.
6. Validate one synthesis and one ascension recipe.
7. Add refining, materials, world complexity, or layering only after playtest evidence.

## Open decisions

- Exact sigil catalog, recipes, tier counts, names, costs, and timings.
- Family-specific machine presentation and how much machinery is shared.
- Synthesis input, buffer, and blockage behavior.
- Socket installation, replacement, removal, consumption, and refund rules.
- Contract difficulty, measurement windows, and simultaneous target count.
- Timing of refining, mixed deposits, world generation, and long-distance logistics.
- Whether special materials change the core appearance or remain internal ingredients.
